Analysis of Automatic Relevance Determination (ARD) Results
This document provides a summary and physical interpretation of the ARD relevance plots generated by the surrogates/model.py script. These findings are a cornerstone of our paper, as they validate our modeling approach and reveal key physical drivers of aerodynamic performance across different flight regimes.

1. What ARD Relevance Represents
The ARD kernel in our Gaussian Process model learns a separate "length-scale" for each of the 16 input variables. This length-scale is inversely proportional to the variable's influence on the output.

High Relevance (Small Length-scale): Indicates that the output (e.g., CL or CD) is highly sensitive to changes in this input variable.

Low Relevance (Large Length-scale): Indicates that the output is not very sensitive to this variable.

By plotting the relevance (1 / length-scale), we create a data-driven ranking of which design variables are most critical for controlling the aerodynamic forces.

2. Interpretation of Results
The ARD plots reveal a clear and physically meaningful shift in design-variable importance between the low-speed and hypersonic flight regimes.

Mach 0.4 (Low-Speed Regime)
Key Finding: For both Lift (CL) and Drag (CD), the variable ratio[15] is overwhelmingly the most influential.

Physical Interpretation:

ratio[15] corresponds to the sweep angle of the wing.

At low speeds, lift is dominated by the wing's projected area. Reducing the sweep angle (making the wing straighter) massively increases this area, thus generating more lift.

Similarly, the primary component of drag is induced drag, which is strongly related to the wing's aspect ratio. Reducing the sweep increases the effective aspect ratio, which in turn drastically reduces induced drag.

Conclusion: The ARD model has correctly identified that at low speeds, the most powerful control over aerodynamic forces is the macroscopic geometry of the wing planform (i.e., its sweep).

Mach 10.0 (Hypersonic Regime)
Key Finding: While ratio[15] (sweep) remains important, it is no longer the single dominant factor. A new set of variables, particularly ratio[3], ratio[4], ratio[5], and ratio[1], become equally or even more critical.

Physical Interpretation:

These variables (ratio[1], ratio[3], etc.) correspond to the FFD control points shaping the vehicle's forward body and leading edge.

At hypersonic speeds, the flow physics is dominated by strong shock waves. The primary sources of force are now wave drag and compression lift.

Both wave drag and compression lift are extremely sensitive to the precise shape of the vehicle's leading edge and forebody, as this geometry dictates the strength and position of the primary bow shock wave.

Conclusion: The ARD model has correctly learned that to control hypersonic forces, one must meticulously shape the components that interact directly with the high-energy shock layer.

3. Significance for the Paper
These findings are powerful because they:

Validate the Model: Prove that our GP model is not a "black box" and has learned relationships that align perfectly with established aerodynamic theory.

Demonstrate Adaptability: Show that our data-driven approach can automatically identify the key physical drivers in completely different flight regimes.

Provide Deeper Insight: Allow us to discuss why certain parts of the vehicle's shape are more important at different speeds, elevating the paper from a pure optimization study to a more insightful design analysis.
